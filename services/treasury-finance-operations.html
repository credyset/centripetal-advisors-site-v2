What the managed finance-operations layer should cover

Centripetal helps founders move from informal cash management to a managed finance-operations layer leadership can use every week. The work follows the company’s actual cash risks, billing handoffs, close cadence, and decision points.

  • Rolling cash forecasting with actuals, collections, payroll, vendors, taxes, and financing obligations.
  • Account structure, signers, payment approvals, wire and ACH controls, and cash positioning.
  • Billing and collections handoffs that connect customer terms to cash timing.
  • Clear ownership for close, treasury, reporting, and the decisions that follow a variance.

Where finance operations usually break

The model and bank balance disagree

Forecasts often use booked revenue or smooth monthly assumptions while the bank account experiences collection delays, payroll dates, annual renewals, taxes, and debt service.

Controls depend on memory

When access, approvals, and payment review live in one person’s head, growth adds operational risk faster than it adds capacity.

Ownership stops at the transaction

Someone posts the bill, someone else runs the model, and the founder answers the board question. A connected finance function makes the handoff explicit.

Frequently asked questions

What does SaaS treasury support include?

Cash positioning, account structure, payment controls, approval workflows, forecasting, financing obligations, and the ownership cadence that keeps the cash view current.

When should a startup formalize treasury controls?

As soon as the company has meaningful cash, multiple signers or accounts, recurring wires or ACH activity, debt obligations, or enough complexity that informal review creates avoidable risk.
