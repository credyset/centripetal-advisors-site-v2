Cash flow and runway planning for B2B SaaS.

Make the cash curve explainable before the next hiring, financing, or board decision.

Direct answer: Cash flow planning is most useful when it connects actual bank movement to the operating choices that can change it. A decision-ready view separates collections, committed spend, hiring, financing obligations, and scenarios so the team can act before runway becomes an emergency.

What the planning cadence should own

Centripetal builds the cash view around the decisions in front of the company, not around a single runway number. The model is reconciled to actuals and reviewed with the people who can change the curve.

  • Weekly cash visibility across collections, payroll, vendors, taxes, and financing obligations.
  • Base, delayed-collections, and downside scenarios tied to named decisions.
  • Hiring and vendor commitments that appear before they hit the bank account.
  • A long-range view that connects runway to fundraising, debt, and growth plans.
  • Owners for each material variance, assumption, and next review.
Runway is not a number to admire. It is a decision window the team has to use.Centripetal operating principle

Where planning usually breaks

Collections are treated as revenue

A signed contract or recognized revenue does not tell leadership when cash will arrive. Payment terms, implementation timing, and collection behavior belong in the cash view.

Committed spend is hidden in smooth lines

Offer letters, annual renewals, tax payments, debt service, and vendor commitments can change the cash curve even when the monthly model looks stable.

Scenarios have no trigger

A base, upside, and downside case only helps when each one has a threshold and an action: slow hiring, change payment terms, revisit financing, or protect a minimum cash level.

Frequently asked questions

What does cash flow planning include for SaaS?

Beginning cash, collections, payroll, vendors, taxes, hiring, financing obligations, committed spend, and the decisions management can make over the next several weeks and months.

When should a SaaS company formalize runway planning?

When cash timing, hiring, collections, financing, or growth choices require more than one simple burn calculation and leadership needs a shared view of tradeoffs.
