Venture debt readiness for B2B SaaS.

Know what the facility needs to accomplish—and what happens if the plan moves against you.

Direct answer: Venture debt readiness means understanding whether the company can support the facility under realistic operating conditions, then preparing the reporting, model, lender materials, and decision rules before the term sheet becomes urgent.

What the readiness work should cover

Centripetal helps founders evaluate debt as part of the capital plan, not as a cheaper-looking substitute for a clear financing strategy. The work starts with the operating need and follows the cash consequences.

  • Reconcile ARR, collections, cash, burn, runway, and debt-service assumptions.
  • Model covenant floors, minimum cash, amortization, and waiver scenarios.
  • Prepare lender-facing financial materials and a defensible data room.
  • Compare debt timing and obligations with the equity plan and operating milestones.
  • Define who owns reporting, covenant monitoring, and the response to a variance.
Rate is priced. Covenants are leverage. Readiness is knowing where that leverage moves before you sign.Centripetal operating principle

Where founders need a clearer answer

What is the debt supposed to fund?

Debt can support a defined bridge, working-capital need, or planned operating milestone. It should not be used to hide a cash plan that the team cannot explain.

What happens when the plan misses?

Stress-test the covenant, cash, and reporting consequences of delayed collections, slower growth, or a hiring change before the lender is the only party with a model.

Who keeps the facility current?

A facility creates an ongoing operating obligation. Assign ownership for reporting dates, covenant calculations, lender communication, and escalation before close.

Frequently asked questions

What does venture debt readiness involve?

Pressure-testing cash capacity, reporting discipline, debt-service capacity, covenant scenarios, lender materials, and the operating decisions that follow a miss or waiver.

Is venture debt right for every SaaS company?

No. Fit depends on predictable cash generation, financing timing, reporting quality, contractual obligations, downside capacity, and whether debt solves a defined operating need.
