The SaaS & AI-Native Finance Scorecard

Eight categories. One score out of forty.

A directional read on the eight categories investors and lenders score when they evaluate your back office. Score yourself honestly. The number is useful. The gaps are more useful.

AT A GLANCE

The SaaS & AI-Native Finance Scorecard is a directional self-assessment for finding back-office gaps before investors, lenders, or board members find them. It is a management tool, not a certification that a company is ready to raise.

Read the full guideLaunch the interactive diagnostic

HOW TO USE THIS

Eight categories. Score each 1–5.

  1. For each of the eight categories below, read the five score descriptions and pick the one that most honestly reflects where you are today. Don’t aspire — describe.
  2. If you’re between two scores, pick the lower one. Investors will read the gap, not the aspiration.
  3. When you finish, the running total updates live. Your total falls between 8 and 40.
  4. The interpretation at the end translates that total into what it means and where to look first. Categories you scored lowest are usually the highest-leverage places to spend the next 30 days.

This is the surface of a 100-question diagnostic we run with every Centripetal client. The categories and scoring scale here mirror the diagnostic’s Scorecard — the punchline artifact we walk through on the first call.

CATEGORY 01

Legal

Entity structure, option plan adoption, employee and advisor agreements, 83(b) elections, FINCEN BOI, IP assignment.

Score for Legal

The basics aren't papered. Missing 83(b) elections, no stock plan formally adopted, employees without PIIA agreements. Each of these is fixable today; each one found in diligence costs a week of explanations.

Diligence teams flag missing 83(b) elections as a tax liability that must be resolved before close. This often delays term sheet execution by weeks.

Entity is set up, but key documents are missing or verbal. Common pattern: option grants approved by email rather than board consent. Investors will ask for the board minutes.

Investors will ask for the full option grant ledger. Verbal grants or email-only approvals create a legal exposure that counsel will flag in the first pass.

The structural pieces are in place, but documentation is uneven. Some employee agreements signed, some verbal. FINCEN BOI may or may not be filed. Cleanup is a few hours of focused work.

Standard package complete: stock plan adopted, agreements signed, 83(b) filings tracked, BOI filed. Edges to refine — advisor agreements, subsidiary documentation if applicable — but no surprises.

Every founder and shareholder has a 83(b) on file. Every employee has signed PIIA. Stock plan adopted via board and stockholder consent. A buyer's lawyer could open this folder cold and find what they need.

CATEGORY 02

Cap Table

Accuracy of current ownership, ability to model forward, option pool sufficiency, treatment of convertible instruments.

Score for Cap Table

Cap table is in a spreadsheet, possibly out of date, with SAFEs and notes that haven't been modeled to conversion. The actual fully-diluted picture isn't knowable in under a day.

Investors model their ownership before making an offer. If the cap table can't produce a reliable fully-diluted view, the conversation pauses until it can.

Current ownership is approximately right, but convertible instruments stack hasn't been modeled. Founders find out their real dilution at term sheet.

A cap table that hasn't modeled SAFE conversion at multiple valuations means the founder doesn't know their own dilution. Investors read that as a governance signal.

Cap table is on Carta or Pulley, current ownership is accurate, but conversion scenarios aren't run. Option pool sufficiency for next year's hires is a guess.

Cap table tracked on a platform, conversion modeled at target valuation, option pool sized against hiring roadmap. Pro forma views ready for an investor conversation.

Cap table is current, fully diluted views run on demand, all instruments modeled at multiple valuation scenarios, option pool right-sized for the next 18 months. Term sheet conversations start with leverage.

CATEGORY 03

Team

Coverage across the 15 finance and back-office functions: bookkeeping, controller, payroll, AR/AP, forecasting, investor relations, treasury, CPA, legal, benefits, billing.

Score for Team

The founder is the bookkeeper, the controller, and the IR contact. Common at pre-seed — but past $1M ARR it's where finance becomes the bottleneck.

Investors probe whether the founder can delegate financial reporting. If the answer is 'I do it all,' that's a scaling risk that shows up in every operating question.

External bookkeeper handles transactions, but no controller-level review, no dedicated AR/AP ownership, forecast updates fall to whoever has time. Single-threaded on the founder.

Diligence teams test whether monthly numbers can be produced without the founder in the room. External bookkeeper plus founder gap usually means the answer is no.

Bookkeeper plus a finance partner or fractional support. Most functions named, but reviewers and approvers aren't always distinct from operators. Internal controls are informal.

Each of the 15 functions has a named owner. Reviewer ≠ doer for monthly close. Investor relations is consistent. Board interface is clean.

Roles documented, succession-aware, dual approvals where they matter. The CFO function (full-time, fractional, or VP Finance) sets the rhythm and the CEO doesn't carry finance details day to day.

CATEGORY 04

Systems

Stack across 11 categories: accounting, expenses, payroll, benefits, banking, HRIS, FP&A, cap table, billing, AP, CRM.

Score for Systems

QuickBooks plus a few spreadsheets, payroll on whatever the bookkeeper recommended, no FP&A tool. Reports take days because data lives in five places.

Investors ask how long it takes to produce a board-ready financial package. If the answer is measured in days, the systems score is visible before anyone asks about it.

The right categories of software are in place, but they don't talk. Cap table on a different platform than payroll and HRIS. Reconciliation is manual.

Partial integration means manual reconciliation. Investors will ask how the cap table reconciles to payroll and equity — if the answer involves spreadsheets, that's the score.

QuickBooks Online or NetSuite, Gusto or Rippling, Carta, Ramp or Brex. Mostly integrated where it matters. Monthly close runs but takes longer than it should.

Stack chosen for fit at this stage, integrations doing real work, FP&A tool generating board-ready views. Founder can pull a current-state snapshot in an hour.

Stack supports the next 12–18 months without major swap-outs. Data flows are documented. New hires onboard onto systems that already work, not ones that need their input to function.

CATEGORY 05

Treasury & Banking

Yield on cash, 2FA on accounts, dual approval on wires, ACH whitelist, credit card program.

Score for Treasury & Banking

Cash earning nothing in a checking account, single approver on wires, no ACH controls. At $2M+ on the balance sheet, this is leaving money and security on the table.

At $2M+ in the bank, earning nothing and running single-approver wires is a finding that shows up in every operational diligence report.

Either some yield or some controls — rarely both. Most companies at this score have moved cash to a money market but haven't tightened wire approvals.

Most companies at this score have yield or controls but not both. Investors notice because it signals which operational details the founder pays attention to.

Yield on cash, 2FA, dual approval on wires above a threshold. ACH whitelist isn't reviewed regularly. Credit card program is informal.

All five controls active, vendor whitelist reviewed quarterly, credit card program with proper expense policy. The kind of operations a Series A investor expects to see.

Treasury policy documented, controls tested, yield strategy reviewed against runway and rate environment. Boring on purpose — and exactly what a sophisticated investor wants.

CATEGORY 06

Accounting

Cash vs. accrual, monthly close speed, reconciliations, revenue recognition, AP/AR processes, expense categorization (R&D / G&A / S&M).

Score for Accounting

Books on cash basis, close happens quarterly at best, no clear revenue recognition policy. The ARR in the deck won't match the P&L when an investor checks.

Cash-basis books at scale force investors to rebuild the P&L on accrual before they can model the business. Some pass rather than do the work.

Books say accrual, but revenue is still recognized at invoice rather than over the contract term. Implementation fees blended with subscription revenue. ASC 606 will surface this in diligence.

Revenue recognized at invoice instead of ratably over the contract term is the single most common accounting finding in Series A diligence.

Accrual basis, monthly close within 15 days, bank reconciliations done. Revenue recognition policy exists but expense category split (R&D / G&A / S&M) is loose.

Monthly close in 5–10 days, all reconciliations current, revenue recognized ratably with services split out. R&D / G&A / S&M tagging is consistent. Stock-based comp accounted for properly.

Books would survive a Big-4 audit with minor adjustments. R&D tax credits captured. Pre-paid expense thresholds and amortization documented. Fixed asset and depreciation schedule current.

CATEGORY 07

Finance

Budget, projections, KPI tracking, investor data package, review cadence, peer benchmarking, capital plan, burn calculation.

Score for Finance

No current-year budget, projections live in a deck from a prior raise, KPIs tracked informally. Burn rate is a guess based on last month's bank balance.

When burn rate is a guess, investors can't model runway. That's not a yellow flag — it's a conversation-ender until the founder can produce a current cash view.

There's a budget, but it isn't reviewed against actuals. Forecasts update when investors ask. KPIs haven't been benchmarked against peers in the last year.

A budget that isn't reviewed against actuals tells investors the forecast is aspirational, not operational. They'll discount every forward projection.

Monthly review of actuals vs. budget, quarterly investor updates with consistent metrics, burn calculated both gross and net. Peer benchmarking happens occasionally.

Monthly review, quarterly reforecast, board-grade investor package, KPIs benchmarked against peer data. Capital plan reflects real timing and scenarios.

Finance is a decision-making input, not a reporting layer. Scenario planning is routine. Pricing, hiring, and channel mix decisions are made with current unit economics in hand.

CATEGORY 08

Investor FAQ Readiness

Ability to answer the 20 questions investors ask after the deck closes: ARR/CARR, NRR, customer concentration, channel mix, sales efficiency, pipeline, NPS, burn, runway.

Score for Investor FAQ Readiness

The deck tells the story, but follow-up questions trigger scrambling. Customer concentration isn't calculated. NRR is named without being measured. Pipeline data lives in someone's head.

If the second-tier metrics require scrambling, investors read it as the founder not knowing their own business. The data might exist — the readiness doesn't.

ARR, growth rate, headcount available. But the second-tier metrics — net sales efficiency, CAC payback, GRR vs. NRR, channel mix — take a week to assemble.

Having ARR and growth but not NRR, concentration, or CAC payback means the founder knows the top line but hasn't interrogated the quality of the revenue.

Standard metrics current, customer concentration tracked, pipeline visible. A few gaps: implementation success rate (CARR-to-ARR conversion), product usage tracking, NPS.

Every question on the standard list has a current-quarter answer with a trend. Customer concentration treated as a real metric. Use of proceeds tied to specific milestones.

Anticipate the questions. Volunteer the awkward ones. Investors leave the meeting with conviction because the founder showed they understand the business at an investor level.

Frequently asked questions

What does the SaaS & AI-Native Finance Scorecard tell me?

It gives a directional view of back-office readiness across eight finance and operating categories. It does not predict fundraising readiness or financing outcomes; it shows where to focus the next cleanup pass.

Who should use it?

Seed and Series A B2B SaaS founders who want a clearer read on legal, cap table, team, systems, treasury, accounting, finance, and investor-question readiness before a board update, lender conversation, or diligence process.

What should I do with a low score?

Start with the lowest-scoring category and name the owner, the missing artifact, and the next 30-day fix. The score matters less than whether the gaps become an operating plan.
