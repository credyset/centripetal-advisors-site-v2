The Pre-Fundraising Financial Checklist Most Founders Skip

The same financial gaps appear in nearly every fundraise we support. They are almost always fixable before the process starts.

Direct answer: Before your first investor meeting, address eight items: revenue recognition, cap table reconciliation, cohort-level retention, unit economics by channel, a 13-week cash forecast, a three-scenario operating model, an organized data room, and board deck alignment with the financial model.

The eight items

1. Revenue recognition audit

Is revenue recognized per ASC 606? Bookings and revenue are not the same, and the distinction matters during diligence. Addressing this early avoids a restatement conversation mid-process.

2. Cap table reconciliation

Every convertible note, SAFE, and option grant should be modeled at the target valuation. The dilution math should produce no surprises when an investor runs the same calculation.

3. Cohort-level retention data

Blended NRR masks segment-level dynamics. We segment by cohort vintage, customer size, and contract type. The trend by segment is more useful than a single blended number.

4. Unit economics by channel

CAC, CAC payback, and LTV:CAC broken out by acquisition channel. Blended metrics hide which channels are efficient and which are subsidized.

5. 13-week cash flow forecast

A forward-looking cash model that accounts for collection timing, not just accrual revenue. This demonstrates that the company understands its actual liquidity position.

6. Three-scenario operating model

Base, upside, and downside cases — each with clearly stated assumptions. One forecast is an estimate. Three forecasts describe a decision framework.

7. Data room organization

Documents organized by diligence category: financials, legal, HR, IP, customer data. A structured data room signals operational maturity before the first conversation.

8. Board deck alignment

The narrative in the pitch deck should reconcile with the numbers in the model. Inconsistencies erode trust faster than weak metrics.

Why preparation matters more than timing

In our experience, the preparation phase is where fundraises succeed or stall — before a single investor meeting. Addressing these gaps early changes the quality of every subsequent conversation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should a SaaS company prepare before fundraising?
Revenue recognition audit, cap table reconciliation, cohort-level retention data, unit economics by channel, a 13-week cash forecast, a three-scenario operating model, an organized data room, and board deck alignment with the financial model.
How long should fundraising preparation take?
In our experience, the preparation phase alone takes one to two months. Starting with 12-14 months of runway remaining gives the company the full timeline without cash pressure affecting negotiating position.
What is the most common fundraising preparation mistake?
Skipping the preparation phase entirely. The same financial gaps — inconsistent revenue recognition, unreconciled cap tables, blended-only retention data — appear repeatedly and are almost always fixable before the process starts.
