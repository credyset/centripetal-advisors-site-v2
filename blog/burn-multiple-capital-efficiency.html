Burn Multiple: The Capital Efficiency Metric That Changes the Conversation

Burn multiple measures how much cash a company spends to generate each dollar of new ARR. It is the capital efficiency question that comes before growth rate in most investor conversations.

Direct answer: Burn multiple is net burn divided by net new ARR. It tells you whether growth is being purchased efficiently or at a rate that consumes capital faster than the business can sustain. Track it monthly and by channel — the blended number hides where spend is actually working.

The formula

Burn Multiple = Net Burn / Net New ARR. It answers a direct question: for every dollar of new annual recurring revenue, how many dollars did the company spend to get it?

The metric is simple. The insight is in the trend and the segmentation.

Why it matters more than growth rate

A company growing quickly with a high burn multiple is purchasing revenue. A company growing steadily with a low burn multiple is building durable value. In our experience, diligence conversations increasingly focus on the relationship between these two numbers rather than growth rate in isolation.

The reason is practical: growth rate tells you how fast the business is expanding. Burn multiple tells you how much capital that expansion is consuming. A business that needs to raise again in nine months to sustain its growth rate is in a fundamentally different position than one that can reach profitability on current capital.

Directional ranges

These are illustrative, not universal thresholds — actual interpretation depends on stage, market, and capital strategy:

Below 1.5x is generally considered efficient. The company is generating ARR without excessive cash consumption. Between 1.5x and 2.0x warrants attention: the spend-to-revenue relationship may not be sustainable through the next raise cycle. Above 2.0x suggests the capital efficiency conversation should happen before, not during, a fundraise.

Segment by channel

We calculate burn multiple monthly for every client — not quarterly, not annually. Monthly cadence catches efficiency drift early, before it becomes a trend that surfaces in the board deck.

We also segment it by channel: paid acquisition burn multiple versus organic. The delta between those two numbers reveals where GTM spend is actually converting into durable revenue and where it is subsidizing growth that would not exist without continued investment.

The self-check

Pull the last three months of net burn and net new ARR. Calculate the ratio for each month. Look at the trend. If the number is climbing, the question is whether the spend driving it is building leverage or consuming runway.

That is the conversation to have before the next raise — not during it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is burn multiple?
Burn multiple is net burn divided by net new ARR. It measures how much cash a company spends to generate each dollar of new annual recurring revenue.
What is a good burn multiple for a SaaS company?
Directional ranges vary by stage and market, but in general, below 1.5x is considered efficient and above 2.0x warrants attention. The metric is most useful when tracked monthly and segmented by channel.
Why does burn multiple matter more than growth rate?
Growth rate alone does not indicate whether the growth is sustainable. Burn multiple connects growth to the capital consumed to produce it, which tells a clearer story about long-term unit economics and capital efficiency.
