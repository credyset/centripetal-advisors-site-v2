Signed Is Not ARR: Why Implementation Velocity Is a Finance Metric

A contract can be signed, but the customer may not be live, billable, collectible, or recognized yet. The finance model should show where the contract actually sits.

Separate signed, live, billable, collectible, and recognized views in management reporting. That keeps implementation timing visible in the forecast, board package, and diligence process without pretending those views are identical.

The gap between signed and live is where forecasts get noisy

Implementation-heavy SaaS companies often create momentum before they create clean financial visibility. Sales closes the deal, the forecast adds the account, and then implementation takes longer than expected, billing starts later, or the customer does not reach full usage until the next period.

That does not automatically mean the sale was bad. It means the model needs to distinguish the stages clearly enough that leadership can manage the delay instead of explaining it after the fact.

Signed-not-live ARR can be a useful management view. It is not an industry-standard metric or an accounting rule.Centripetal reporting guardrail

The useful view separates four questions

  1. What is signed? Contract value committed by the customer.
  2. What is live? The customer is implemented and using the product.
  3. What is billable or collectible? The contract has reached the point where invoices and cash timing matter.
  4. What is recognized? Accounting treatment determined under the company’s revenue policy.

SaaS contracts differ. Implementation, billing, payment, usage, and recognition can move differently depending on the product and contract terms. The point is traceability, not a universal sequence.

Why diligence teams probe this

We see diligence teams probe the bridge between bookings, ARR, CARR, recognized revenue, cash collections, and customer-level exports. They are testing whether management can explain movement from contract to implementation to the financial statements without changing definitions in the middle of the conversation.

  • Which signed customers are not live yet?
  • How long does implementation take by segment?
  • When does billing start, and when does cash arrive?
  • Which customers are included in ARR, CARR, pipeline, and recognized revenue?
  • Where would a delayed go-live create a board-plan miss?

Implementation velocity belongs in the finance model

Implementation velocity affects cash planning, sales capacity, revenue visibility, board reporting, and how much new business the company can absorb without creating delivery risk. A useful first view can include the signed customer, contract start date, expected and actual go-live dates, billing start, first invoice, collection timing, management ARR/CARR treatment, and accounting treatment under the revenue policy.

What this changes in board reporting

A board update that shows only ARR growth can hide implementation drag. A clearer package separates live ARR, signed-not-live ARR, new bookings, implementation backlog, cash collection timing, and recognized revenue.

If signed demand is strong but implementation is slow, the conversation is about delivery capacity and onboarding. If implementation is fast but collections lag, the conversation is about payment terms and accounts receivable. If recognized revenue differs from the management ARR view, the conversation belongs with the accountant and revenue policy.

Frequently asked questions

Does signed SaaS revenue count as ARR before implementation?

It depends on the company’s management definitions and accounting policy. For operating purposes, separate signed, live, billable, and recognized views so implementation timing remains visible.

Is signed-not-live ARR an industry-standard metric?

No. It is a management-reporting view that isolates contracted customer value that has not yet become live operating revenue in the model.

Is this revenue-recognition advice?

No. Revenue-recognition treatment belongs with the company’s accountant and accounting policy. This field note addresses management reporting and forecast reconciliation.
