The Finance Review Before Your First AE Hire

The first AE should multiply a sales motion you understand. The finance review is how you test whether the company is ready to fund the learning curve.

Hire the first AE when the founder has a repeatable sales motion, understands the buyer and cycle, and can hand over a playbook specific enough to test. Before signing the offer, model the ramp, compensation, hiring date, pipeline assumptions, onboarding capacity, and cash impact.

The hire is a multiplication decision

Founders often want the first AE because they want to stop selling. That is the wrong trigger. Early sales are still a source of customer learning, and an AE hired before the motion is clear can create activity without creating repeatable learning.

The better question is whether the founder can explain what the AE is being asked to repeat: who buys, why they buy, how the process moves, where deals stall, and what a qualified opportunity looks like.

The first AE should multiply a motion you can teach—not become the experiment that discovers whether the motion exists.Centripetal hiring principle

Run the finance review in six parts

1. Sales motion

Write the sequence from first conversation to close. Include the buyer, champion, proof points, objections, sales cycle, and founder involvement. If this is still changing every week, call it a test—not a repeatable motion.

2. Ramp and capacity

Define what the AE will learn, who will coach them, and which implementation or customer-success resources support new business. A capacity gap can make a sales hire look unproductive when the constraint is elsewhere.

3. Compensation

Model base pay, variable compensation, onboarding costs, benefits, recruiting fees, and the timing of commissions. Keep the assumptions visible so the plan can be adjusted without rebuilding the model.

4. Pipeline reality

Separate founder-created opportunities from opportunities the AE is expected to create. Show the stage, timing, conversion assumptions, and the difference between a hopeful pipeline and one the team can explain.

5. Cash and runway

Model the full expense before the first incremental cash arrives. Test a slower ramp, a later start, and the possibility that the founder must continue carrying part of the motion longer than planned.

6. Decision milestones

Set the checkpoints that change the plan: hiring, territory, compensation, coaching, or a pause. The milestone should measure learning and pipeline quality as well as closed business.

What the board should be able to see

A decision-ready hiring plan connects the role to the operating model. It shows why now, what the founder has already learned, what the AE will own, which assumptions drive cash, and what evidence will determine whether the plan is working.

Keep the review generalized to the decision. Customer-specific details, company names, and active-client assumptions do not belong in a reusable hiring framework.

Frequently asked questions

When is the right time to hire a first AE?

When the founder has a repeatable sales motion, understands the buyer and sales cycle, and can hand an AE a playbook that is specific enough to test and improve.

What should a finance review include before an AE hire?

Review the sales motion, ramp plan, base and variable compensation, hiring timing, pipeline assumptions, onboarding capacity, cash impact, and the milestones that would change the plan.

Should an AE own founder-led sales immediately?

Not automatically. The transition should match the evidence behind the sales motion, the founder’s ability to coach the process, and the company’s capacity to absorb a learning period.
