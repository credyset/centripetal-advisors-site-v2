Scenario Modeling Is a Decision System, Not Three Tabs

A scenario model is useful when it changes the decisions management is prepared to make—not when it simply adds more tabs.

Direct answer: Use a small set of cases to show the range of outcomes, the trigger that matters, and the action available before the constraint arrives. The objective is not to assign probabilities; it is to act earlier when a material driver moves.

What a single scenario hides

A single forecast can make every assumption look equally certain. It also gives the board no clear way to discuss what happens if sales cycles lengthen, collections slip, hiring accelerates, or a financing closes later than planned.

Scenarios make the model a decision instrument. They reveal which assumptions matter most, how quickly cash changes, and which levers are reversible.

Build three cases around a small set of drivers

Base case

Use the operating plan the team is currently managing to. It should be tied to actual conversion, hiring timing, pricing, collections, and known commitments—not a wish list.

Upside case

Show what must go right for the company to outperform. Identify the capacity, investment, and cash required to support that outcome. Upside is not free revenue.

Downside case

Change only the drivers that could plausibly move: pipeline conversion, churn, collection timing, hiring pace, or financing timing. Then connect the result to specific actions and trigger dates.

The most useful downside case is not the most dramatic one. It is the one that gives the team enough warning to still have choices.Centripetal operating principle

Turn scenarios into a cadence

  • Review the drivers that changed since the prior forecast.
  • Show the cash and runway effect, not only the revenue effect.
  • Assign an owner to each decision that could change the curve.
  • Set trigger dates for hiring, spend controls, financing, or customer intervention.
  • Retire scenarios that no longer reflect the actual decision set.

Frequently asked questions

Which scenarios should a SaaS model include?

A base case, an upside case, and a downside case are usually enough when each has explicit assumptions, trigger points, and management actions.

What makes a downside case useful?

A useful downside case changes a small number of material drivers, shows the cash impact, and names the actions available before the company reaches the constraint.
