Rule of 40: The Series B Ceiling You Can See From Series A

Growth equity investors use Rule of 40 as the first filter. Most Series A founders don't realize it's already shaping the ceiling for their next round.

Direct answer: Rule of 40 combines revenue growth rate and profit margin. It is the efficiency benchmark growth equity and Series B investors use to evaluate whether a company can sustain both. Start tracking it at Series A — because once ARR is high enough that growth rate alone cannot carry the number, only operating discipline closes the gap.

What Rule of 40 measures

The formula is simple: revenue growth rate plus profit margin. If a company grows 60% year over year with a negative 20% profit margin, its Rule of 40 score is 40. If it grows 30% with a 15% margin, the score is 45.

The metric does not prescribe a ratio between growth and efficiency. It tests whether the company can deliver a combination that adds up to a threshold investors consider fundable at the growth-equity stage.

Why it matters at Series A

Series A investors care about growth rate, market size, and product-market fit. Series B investors care about those things too — but they also care about the path to efficiency. Rule of 40 is the framework they use to evaluate that path.

If a company raises its Series A at 100% growth with a negative 40% margin, its Rule of 40 score is 60. That looks strong. But growth rates decelerate. If the company reaches $10M ARR and growth slows to 40%, the margin needs to reach breakeven to maintain Rule of 40. That is a structural shift in how the company operates.

You cannot grow your way to Rule of 40 at scale. You have to earn your way there through operating efficiency — and that takes quarters of discipline, not a single quarter of cost-cutting.Centripetal operating principle

The two levers

Growth is the lever founders understand. It is the narrative of the pitch deck. But the margin lever is where Rule of 40 is usually won or lost at the Series B stage.

Margin improvements come from gross margin expansion (reducing COGS, improving delivery efficiency), operating leverage (growing revenue faster than headcount), and capital efficiency (reducing burn multiple, improving CAC payback). These are not one-quarter projects. They are operating system decisions that compound over time.

How to track it

Calculate Rule of 40 monthly, starting at Series A close. Use trailing twelve-month revenue growth and the corresponding profit margin. Watch the trend, not any single month.

If the score is above 40 and trending up, the Series B story writes itself. If the score is declining — even from a high starting point — that is the signal to investigate which side of the equation is weakening and whether the operating plan addresses it.

What to bring to the next board meeting

  • Trailing twelve-month Rule of 40 score and the three-quarter trend.
  • The decomposition: what portion comes from growth and what comes from margin.
  • The operating plan assumptions about how each side changes over the next four quarters.
  • The specific decisions — hiring pace, pricing, channel mix, cost structure — that move the margin lever.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Rule of 40 in SaaS?

The Rule of 40 says that a SaaS company's revenue growth rate plus its profit margin should equal or exceed 40%. It is a combined measure of growth and efficiency that growth equity investors use as a screening threshold.

When should a SaaS founder start tracking Rule of 40?

At Series A. The metric becomes the ceiling for the Series B conversation, and it takes quarters of operating discipline to move it. Starting to track it after the Series A closes gives the team time to understand the tradeoff between growth rate and margin.

Can you grow your way to Rule of 40?

At lower ARR levels, high growth rates can carry the number. As ARR scales, growth rates naturally decelerate, and the margin side of the equation becomes the primary lever. Companies that reach $10M+ ARR usually need efficiency gains — not just growth — to sustain Rule of 40.
