The Unit Economics Framework for SaaS: Four Layers, by Segment

Unit economics is the foundation of every financial conversation with SaaS founders. Most companies calculate these metrics incompletely — or only in aggregate.

SaaS unit economics has four layers: customer acquisition cost, gross margin per customer, payback and lifetime value, and efficiency. Each layer should be calculated by segment, by channel, and by cohort — not blended. The segmented view is where strategic decisions about acquisition, pricing, and investment actually live.

Layer 1: Customer acquisition

Fully loaded CAC = (Sales + Marketing spend + allocated overhead) / New customers acquired.

Most founders undercount by excluding sales salaries, tooling, and allocated G&A. In our experience, the actual CAC is often materially higher than the number being reported, because the denominator includes only the direct marketing spend.

Layer 2: Gross margin per customer

Gross margin per customer = (Revenue - COGS per customer) / Revenue.

COGS should include hosting, support headcount, onboarding costs, and any professional services. When gross margins fall below the level expected for a software business, the question is whether the company is pricing like software but delivering like services.

Layer 3: Payback and lifetime value

CAC payback = Fully loaded CAC / (Monthly ARPU x Gross Margin %). LTV = ARPU x Gross Margin % x (1 / Monthly Churn Rate).

The LTV:CAC ratio is directional, not a pass/fail. In general, a ratio above 3:1 suggests sustainable acquisition; below that level, the economics of the acquisition motion warrant investigation. Above 5:1 may indicate the company could invest more aggressively in growth.

Layer 4: Efficiency

Burn multiple = Net Burn / Net New ARR. Magic number = Net New ARR / Prior Quarter Sales & Marketing Spend. These efficiency metrics connect growth to the capital consumed to produce it.

The segmentation imperative

These metrics need to be calculated by segment — not blended. Enterprise, mid-market, and SMB customers have fundamentally different acquisition costs, margins, and retention dynamics. A blended LTV:CAC that looks healthy can mask an enterprise segment creating significant value and an SMB segment that is not. The segmented view is where the actionable decisions live.

Frequently asked questions

What are the four layers of SaaS unit economics?

Customer acquisition (fully loaded CAC), gross margin per customer, payback and lifetime value (CAC payback period and LTV:CAC), and efficiency (burn multiple and magic number).

Why should unit economics be calculated by segment?

Blended metrics hide segment-level problems. Enterprise, mid-market, and SMB customers have fundamentally different acquisition costs, margins, and retention dynamics. Segmented unit economics reveal where the business is creating or destroying value.

What is fully loaded CAC?

Fully loaded CAC includes sales and marketing spend plus allocated overhead — salaries, tooling, and G&A — divided by new customers acquired. Most founders undercount by excluding these costs, which understates the true acquisition cost.
