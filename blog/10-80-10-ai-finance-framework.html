The 10-80-10 Framework: How Finance Teams Should Use AI

10% scoping. 80% AI execution. 10% human judgment review. The framework is not about replacing finance people — it is about making the finance function faster without making it wrong.

Direct answer: AI is useful for extraction, categorization, reconciliation, and pattern surfacing in finance. It does not replace the judgment that determines what the data means and which operating response to choose. The 10-80-10 framework separates scoping (human), execution (AI), and review (human judgment).

The real risk is not wrong output

The dangerous AI failure mode in finance is not that the tool produces wrong output — it is that the team accepts AI output without reviewing it, and the error shows up in a board deck or diligence package.

AI tools are fast. They can categorize transactions, extract data from contracts, reconcile accounts, and surface anomalies across large datasets. But speed without review creates a new kind of risk: confident errors at scale.

The 10-80-10 breakdown

10% — Human scoping

Before AI touches the work, a human defines the task. What data goes in. What output is expected. What the output will be used for. The scoping step is where errors are prevented — a poorly defined task produces a confidently wrong result.

80% — AI execution

The middle portion is where AI earns its value. Transaction categorization. Data extraction from contracts and invoices. Reconciliation across systems. Pattern recognition across months or cohorts. These are high-volume, rule-based tasks where speed matters and the error cost of individual items is low.

10% — Human judgment review

The final step is where the finance function earns its credibility. A human reviews the output, validates the categorizations, checks the edge cases, and decides what the numbers mean. This is not a rubber stamp — it is the step where judgment is applied.

Where the framework applies

  • Monthly close: AI categorizes transactions and flags anomalies. A human reviews the categorizations and investigates the flags.
  • Revenue recognition: AI extracts contract terms. A human applies the recognition rules and validates the schedule.
  • Variance analysis: AI identifies the largest variances and surfaces contributing factors. A human interprets what the variances mean and recommends a response.
  • Diligence preparation: AI compiles and organizes data room documents. A human validates accuracy and ensures the narrative is defensible.

Where AI does not belong

AI should not make judgment calls about what a variance means, which operating response to choose, how to explain a number to a board, or how to allocate capital. These decisions require context about the business, the team, the market, and the strategy that AI does not have.

The framework is not anti-AI. It is anti-laziness. The finance function's value is judgment, and judgment cannot be delegated to a tool — only the work that feeds judgment can be accelerated.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 10-80-10 AI framework?

10% scoping (human defines the task), 80% AI execution (extraction, categorization, pattern surfacing), 10% review (human judgment validates). The framework separates what AI does well from what requires human judgment.

Where does AI add the most value in SaaS finance?

Transaction categorization, anomaly detection, reconciliation, data extraction, and pattern recognition across large datasets. These are high-volume, rule-based tasks where speed matters and the error cost of individual items is low.

Where does AI fail in finance?

Judgment calls: interpreting what a variance means, choosing an operating response, explaining a number to a board or investor, and making capital allocation decisions. These require context that AI does not have.
