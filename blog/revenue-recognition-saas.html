Revenue Recognition for SaaS: What Founders Get Wrong

Revenue recognition is one of the most misunderstood areas of SaaS finance. These mistakes surface during diligence and can derail a fundraise if they are not corrected before the process starts.

Direct answer: Revenue recognition under ASC 606 requires matching revenue to when the performance obligation is satisfied, not when a contract is signed or an invoice is sent. Getting this wrong inflates current-period financials and creates reconciliation problems that diligence teams will find.

Signed does not mean recognized

Under ASC 606, revenue is recognized when the performance obligation is satisfied — meaning when the service is delivered, not when the contract is signed. A 12-month contract signed in January for $120K should generally be recognized at $10K per month over the contract term, not $120K in January.

Recognizing revenue upfront inflates current-period revenue and creates a deferred revenue liability on the balance sheet. In our experience, diligence teams restate financials when they find this pattern.

ARR and recognized revenue are not the same thing

ARR — Annual Recurring Revenue — is an operating metric. It annualizes the value of recurring contracts. Recognized revenue is an accounting measure under GAAP. They serve different purposes and often produce different numbers.

ARR is forward-looking: what the business is contracted for today, annualized. Recognized revenue is backward-looking: what was earned and delivered in a given period. Presenting ARR as revenue in financial statements creates audit risk and diligence friction.

Services revenue should not be bundled with subscriptions

Implementation fees, onboarding charges, and consulting services are typically separate performance obligations under ASC 606. They should be recognized as delivered and reported separately from subscription revenue.

Bundling services into subscription revenue inflates gross margins. Diligence teams segment these revenue streams because the expected margins differ — subscription gross margins are typically higher than services margins. When the split is not clean, the conversation slows down.

Usage-based revenue follows its own rules

Usage-based revenue follows variable consideration rules under ASC 606. Recognition depends on the specific contract terms and measurement period — not the invoice date. The distinction matters for companies with consumption-based pricing, metered APIs, or usage tiers.

In our experience, this is the area where founders are most likely to have a reasonable approach that simply does not match the accounting standard. A conversation with an accountant familiar with ASC 606 is worth having early.

The credibility test

Revenue recognition is not just an accounting exercise. It is a credibility exercise. In our diligence work, we see teams probe whether revenue is recognized correctly before they evaluate anything else in the financial story. Getting it wrong undermines the numbers, the narrative, and the trust that makes a financing conversation productive.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should a SaaS company recognize revenue under ASC 606?
Revenue is recognized when the performance obligation is satisfied — meaning when the service is delivered, not when the contract is signed. A 12-month contract should generally be recognized ratably over the service period.
Is ARR the same as recognized revenue?
No. ARR is an operating metric that annualizes recurring contract value. Recognized revenue is an accounting measure under GAAP. They serve different purposes and often produce different numbers.
Should professional services revenue be bundled with subscription revenue?
Generally no. Implementation fees, onboarding, and consulting are typically separate performance obligations under ASC 606. They should be recognized as delivered and reported separately, because the expected gross margins differ.
