The Real Timeline of a Series A — and Why Most Founders Underestimate It

The single most consistent mistake we see: founders underestimate how long the process takes. The preparation phase is where rounds succeed or stall.

A Series A process typically takes five to six months from preparation to close, and three to four months from first investor meeting to close. The difference between fundraising from a position of strength and negotiating under pressure is often just planning.

Months 1-2: Preparation

This phase is often skipped. It includes: financial model rebuild and stress-testing, data room construction, cap table reconciliation and conversion modeling, board deck refinement, narrative development, and pitch rehearsal.

In our experience, the preparation phase is where rounds are won or lost — before a single investor meeting happens.

Month 3: Outreach and first meetings

A target list of investors prioritized by fit. First meetings scheduled. Initial feedback collected and the narrative adjusted. This is the phase where the pitch starts meeting the market.

Month 4: Deep dives and partner meetings

Second meetings with interested firms. Partner meeting presentations. Preliminary diligence questions begin. The number of active conversations narrows to those with genuine fit.

Month 5: Term sheets and diligence

Term sheets received and negotiated. Full diligence process — financial, legal, technical, customer. A clean data room can save significant time in this phase.

Month 6: Close

Legal documentation and final negotiation. Wire and close.

The cash implication

If a founder plans for a three-month process and it takes six, the difference is three additional months of runway consumed. At any meaningful burn rate, that delta changes the company's negotiating position. The difference between fundraising from a position of strength and negotiating under cash pressure is often just the preparation timeline.

We recommend starting preparation when the company has 12-14 months of runway remaining. That gives the full timeline without cash pressure affecting the outcome.
